# Revised study preparation and M1 pilot

Completed E0 and legacy M3 are skipped. Their saved responses remain available
and all280 M3 responses now have sandbox evaluation records. The offline audit
prints counts, settings and hashes; draft matrices expose actual call budgets.
New builders separate active/passive/filler tasks, five memory policies and
validated semantic distractors. They do not invoke Ollama.

The owner approved the fixed40 roster and a70-call diagnostic M1 pilot. Exact
prompts, seeded companions and partial target-slot balance are published.
Controls match current M1 settings; old temperature0 alone is insufficient.
70 calls are generated sequentially, then scored separately, then execution
stops. No pilot row enters main hypothesis tests. R1 summaries, semantic
variants and later stages still need separate materials and registration.

Key files: memory_revision.py, prepare_memory_revision.py, prepare_m1_pilot.py,
validate_m1_pilot.py, run_m1_pilot.py and docs/PROMPTS_M1_PILOT.md.

Five questions:
1. Why can a sampled E0 response not serve as a deterministic M1 control?
2. Why do active bundles have a larger output cap?
3. How does target correctness differ from whole-bundle success?
4. Why are pilot results excluded from hypothesis tests?
5. Why does oracle selective retention not establish practical retrieval quality?

Assumptions: pilot grid approval permits the documented routine seed/order
implementation, not a claim of individually reviewed prompts. Fresh sensor
logging and approved host state are verified before calls. No causal conclusion
or model knowledge claim follows from preparation or diagnostic results.

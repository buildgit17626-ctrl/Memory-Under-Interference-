# M3 · Load versus relevance · 2026-11-17

Status: preregistration with explicitly unresolved stimulus choices.
## Hypothesis from the owner's briefing
C1 predicts lower correctness as context load and relevant interference rise
before the context limit. Compare irrelevant filler, other tasks and
near-duplicate tasks at matched total prompt lengths and matched target
position. Competing predictions are no detectable reduction within this range,
or a similar reduction for irrelevant and relevant filler.

## Conditions and outcomes
Catalogue levels are 500/1000/2000/4000 total input tokens and target
start/middle/end. The Focused allocation permits two lengths and one position,
six cells per task, 20 independently eligible tasks per model (240 calls).
A proposal of 1000/4000 tokens and middle position is awaiting owner approval.
The rendering must count the complete system/chat template and target.
Filler and target tests are frozen; failed matching or context overflow is
invalid, never replaced by a differently sized prompt after seeing success.

Primary outcome is target base-plus pass rate. The primary paired contrast is
near-duplicate minus irrelevant filler, averaged over matched length cells
within each task and then over tasks, per model. Secondary reports show all
cells, latency, tokens, and cost. This operationalises C1 without equating a
context-window size to an intrinsic cognitive capacity.
## Analysis and interpretation-changers
Plot task-paired correctness and differences by length. If all filler types
decline together, token load remains an alternative explanation to semantic
interference. Input loss, target-position differences, budget caps or a
handful of item templates dominating the result would qualify interpretation.
The first 100 logical M3 requests trigger the mandatory timing checkpoint,
including invalid requests in workload counts and disclosing them separately.
